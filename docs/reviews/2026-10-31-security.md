## Medium

### M1. A passkey confirmation passes step-up, and the passkey that gives it can be registered in the 3-hour window

- **Where:**
  - `billing/config/fortify.php:172-174` enables `Features::passkeys()`. Fortify then registers
    `POST /passkeys/confirm` (`Laravel\Passkeys\Http\Controllers\PasskeyConfirmationController::store`), which calls
    `$session->passwordConfirmed()` and so sets `auth.password_confirmed_at`.
  - `billing/app/Http/Middleware/RequireRecentPasswordConfirmation.php:134-160` (`recentlyConfirmed()` and
    `confirmedFor()`) accepts that timestamp for any login that has a password.
  - Registering a passkey (`GET /user/passkeys/options`, `POST /user/passkeys`) and the two-factor routes (disable,
    recovery codes, secret key) use Laravel's `password.confirm` (`vendor/laravel/fortify/routes/routes.php:185-187`,
    `:146-147`). Its window is `auth.password_timeout`, which is 10800 s (`billing/config/auth.php:115`), not
    `auth.step_up_seconds` (900 s).
- **Impact:** ADR 0018 §4 says the timestamp is set "only" by Fortify's confirm-password. A passkey may count only
  through an amendment, and none exists.
  - Attack: someone takes a session up to 3 hours after the owner last typed their password. They do not know the
    password. They register their own passkey, then call `/passkeys/confirm` with it whenever they need step-up.
  - That passes every `password.recent` gate, including email change (account takeover), gateway, server and
    registrar secrets, roles and staff, Show password, and the mail server. It never touches the #522 guess budget.
  - No mail tells the owner: nothing listens for `PasskeyRegistered`.
  - The same 3-hour window lets the session turn two-factor off or read the recovery codes.
  - The passkey assertion itself is sound: user verification is required, the credential is bound to the user, and the
    challenge is single-use. So the gap is the policy and the window, not WebAuthn.
- **Reproduction:** `StepUpPasskeyProbesTest`. Red on SQLite and on PostgreSQL.
  - **S1:** registration options with a confirmation from an hour ago give 200; the safe answer is 423.
  - **S2:** a passkey confirmation followed by a `password.recent` route gives 204; the safe answer is 423. The probe
    replaces `VerifyPasskey`, so it exercises the controller and middleware path, not a real signature.
  - **S3:** two-factor disable with a confirmation from an hour ago gives 200; the safe answer is 423.
  - **S4** holds: signing in, including the two-factor step, sets no confirmation.
- **Fix:** pick one.
  1. Disable the passkey confirmation route, or stop it setting `auth.password_confirmed_at`. Leave ADR 0018 as written.
  2. Amend ADR 0018 to accept passkeys. In the same change:
     - gate passkey registration and every two-factor change with `password.recent`, not `password.confirm`, or set
       `auth.password_timeout` to `auth.step_up_seconds`;
     - send a security notice when a passkey is added or removed;
     - audit passkey confirmations.

  Either way, add the passkey and two-factor routes to `SensitiveRouteStepUpTest`.

### M2. Inbound support mail is attached to an account email nobody verified

- **Where:**
  - `billing/app/Modules/Support/Services/RequesterResolver.php:19-41` (`match()`) attaches the mail when any of these
    equals the sender:
    - the account email (`clients.email`), whoever set it;
    - a login's email, verified or not;
    - a contact's email.
  - `isAuthorizedSender()` (`:70-87`) uses the same rule for email replies.
  - A signed-in client can set the account email to any address: `AccountDetailsRequest.php:28-30`, saved with
    `EmailSource::Client` and not verified. Sign-up and checkout registration also store an unverified address
    (`CreateNewUser`, `RegisterCheckoutClient`).
- **Impact:** this is the recurring class "an email address treated as proof of ownership".
  1. A client sets their account email to `someone@victim.example`.
  2. A person with that address and no account of their own emails support, for example about a domain transfer.
     The ticket opens on the attacker's account.
  3. The attacker reads it under Support. Staff also see the sender as a contact of that account, and later email
     replies from the address count as authorised.

  `EmailSource::isVouched()` already exists for exactly this decision. KYC and the account import use it; the support
  matcher does not. Panel contacts are entered by staff, so they are vouched for. C2, C3 and C4 pin that the rest of
  the matching fails safe.
- **Reproduction:** `SupportInboundProbesTest` C1, red on SQLite and on PostgreSQL. It sets the email through
  `PUT client/account`, then sends an inbound email from that address. It expects the ticket's `client_id` to be null
  and gets the attacker's client.
- **Fix:**
  1. In `match()` and `isAuthorizedSender()`, count an account email only when it is vouched (`email_source` staff or
     import) or when a login of that client verified the same address.
  2. Count a login only when `email_verified_at` is set.
  3. Contacts stay as they are.
  4. Mail that would have matched an unvouched address opens a guest ticket.

## Low

### L1. The SMTP relay check runs at save and at test send, not at connect (defence in depth)

`MailRelayHost` (relay profile, ADR 0015 amendment #532) resolves the host when the settings are saved, and
`MailTestController` checks again before a test send. Queued mail connects through Symfony's own resolver, so it is
not pinned to the IP that was checked. A host name whose DNS later points at `169.254.169.254` or another refused
range passes. This needs a sensitive permission plus step-up, and an SMTP dialogue gets little out of an HTTP metadata
service, so the impact is low.

**Fix:** resolve and check the host in the transport factory (or in `MailServerSettings::apply()`), and connect to the
checked IP with `peer_name` set for TLS.

Probe M1 in `UploadAndMailProbesTest` holds. It pins the parts that work today:
- a metadata host is refused at save;
- a partial save that leaves out `mail.default` (which `MailRelayHost` reads) is refused as a whole.

### L2. The test send reports internal hosts and ports (accepted by design, note only)

The relay profile accepts loopback and private addresses, and the test send shows the server's error text (up to
1000 bytes, credentials masked). So a holder of `core.settings.mail` can tell an open internal port from a closed one,
and read a banner. ADR 0015 accepts this on purpose, because a LAN relay is normal. Keep the permission sensitive and
the 5-per-10-minutes limit. A `Log only` mailer also writes reset links to the application log: same permission, same
note.

## Verified secure

- **Step-up and the #522 budget.**
  - The budget applies to every password check while signed in: `confirm-password`, which also serves every
    `password.confirm` page; password change; and account deletion.
  - Each wrong confirmation is audited (`auth.confirm_password_failed`).
  - Signing in, including the two-factor challenge, never sets `auth.password_confirmed_at` (S4).
  - A login without a password, or with an expired one-time password, is never confirmed.
  - Impersonation clears the confirmation.
  - Passkey assertions require user verification, are bound to the signed-in user (`ensurePasskeyBelongsToUser`), use
    a single-use challenge (`session()->pull`) and are throttled (`passkeys`, 10 a minute).
  - The two-factor challenge is throttled at 5 a minute per pending login.
- **#520.**
  - `invite()` refuses an existing login that has not verified its address.
  - Password reset rotates the remember token and revokes every session; a password change keeps only the current one.
  - Invitations carry a fingerprint of the password hash (hidden on the model), so any later password write voids the
    link.
  - The admin page receives only booleans derived from the password and two-factor columns, never the columns.
- **#532 mail server.**
  - `core.settings.mail` is in the sensitive tier, so `core.settings.*` does not grant it.
  - Saving needs step-up, checked before validation, and the refusal carries no old input. Full administrators get a
    security notice.
  - The SMTP password and the SES key and secret are stored encrypted and sent to the page as `null` plus `is_set`.
    The audit diff masks them.
  - The test send needs step-up and is limited to 5 per staff member per 10 minutes, with a 20-second deadline.
  - The test mail has fixed text, and the staff name in it is escaped.
  - Errors are masked against every credential in effect, including a password inside `MAIL_URL`.
  - The host is restricted to `[A-Za-z0-9.\-:\[\]]` and judged by the kernel guard, which refuses link-local,
    metadata, reserved and IPv6-embedded forms.
- **Importers.**
  - There is no WHMCS API importer yet. `MigrationSource` supports `csv` only, so there is no source URL to point at
    an internal host and no stored remote credential.
  - The arch rule added in #527 forbids `Http`, the bus and queue contracts, and `dispatch_sync()` in importers.
  - The importers are `ClientImporter`, `LoginImporter` and `OpeningBalanceImporter`. Each writes a closed field set
    (`ClientColumns::TARGETS`), and the opening balance takes only an integer amount of zero or more in the client's
    own currency.
  - The linked client comes from the run's own id map. `SourceIds` filters every query by `migration_source_id`, so
    one source cannot resolve another source's ids. The #527 `targetIds()` keeps that filter.
  - Imported text (notes, imported fields) is rendered by React as text. The only raw-HTML sinks in `resources/js`
    are the 2FA QR SVG (server-generated), the chart style block, and the support Markdown renderer.
  - The errors CSV goes through `SafeCsv`.
  - The upload is limited by extension, size, rows and columns, encrypted onto the objects disk under a UUID, and its
    temporary copy is deleted.
  - Every write route needs the sensitive `migration.runs.execute`, step-up and a throttle.
- **File uploads.**
  - Support attachments pass only when the extension is in a closed `MIME_MAP` and finfo agrees with it. So html,
    xhtml, xml, shtml or svg are refused even after staff add them to the allow-list (U1). The hard deny list covers
    double extensions.
  - Stored names are random under `support/tickets/<id>/`. Original names are reduced to `[A-Za-z0-9._-]` with
    `basename()`, so no path survives (U2).
  - Downloads send `nosniff`, a sandbox CSP and `no-store`. A file is shown inline only when it is an image, never a
    PDF (U2).
  - The ticket is scoped by `ClientContext` and the attachment by ticket id (U3).
  - Inbound email attachments go through the same rule.
  - Brand uploads accept raster images only (SVG refused), use an extension guessed from the content, live on the
    private disk, and are served with a type fixed by extension, `nosniff` and `default-src 'none'`.
- **Sub-user and documents (tested, not read).**
  - A login with every area but `billing` gets 403 on credit and debit note PDFs (GET and HEAD), the invoice PDF and
    the quote PDF, and no print is counted (D1, D3).
  - A login with `billing` on account A and `support` on account B gets 404 for B's note while A is selected, and 403
    once B is selected, for the list too (D2).
- **Panel contacts in inbound mail.**
  - Contacts are created only by staff (`SaveContact`).
  - An address on two accounts is ambiguous and opens a guest ticket (C2).
  - A contact of another account replying threaded on this account's ticket is blocked (C3).
  - A matched contact never gets a login (C4).
  - A reply needs both an authorised sender and threading on one of our Message-IDs.
- **Workflows:** unchanged since 10-29; the findings of that review stand.
