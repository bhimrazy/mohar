## 4. Risks to the business

Go-live blockers (Nepali host, under 2,000 clients):

- **Nepal tax compliance is unexamined.** IRD's electronic-billing rules require VAT-registered businesses that bill
  by software to use listed software that reports to CBMS; the exact scope for a small host needs an accountant's
  answer. Nothing in the product docs mentions CBMS; the roadmap lists it as wave 4 with no date. If it applies, a
  VAT-registered host cannot legally issue tax invoices from the product. This is the one item that can make the
  product unusable in the chosen market regardless of features.
- **Payments proven only in part.** Khalti has no live run; eSewa's happy path waits on the owner; a declined card
  leaves the client stuck (#123). All three are days or less to close and all three are visible to the host's customers.
- **Server cost.** 6–8 GB minimum. Most Nepali hosts will try it on a 2–4 GB VPS or shared cPanel and fail on day one.
- **Unchanged from round 2:** #25 key rotation, #64 shared lock store, backup and restore untested, branch protection
  unverified (owner), one shared GitHub account, the 10-22 framework security bump (#11).
- **.np documents are citizenship and company scans.** #168 encrypts them at rest, limits downloads to a signed URL plus a
  permission, and deletes them after 30 days. Keep that bar in the screens PR; it is the most sensitive data the
  product will hold in Nepal.

Owner dependencies, in order of lead time: NCHL enrolment for connectIPS (weeks, start now); bank decision for NPR cards
(Q4, Q10 in #128); eSewa CAPTCHA run and Khalti sandbox run (hours); licence and price model in NPR; support model;
trademark search for "Mohar" in Nepal (Department of Industry) and India; branch protection; security policy; a first
host or design partner in Kathmandu.

Verification of the WHMCS importer without a licence bypass: the "WHMCS clients export" preset's headers are marked
unverified in the spec, and the API connector has never seen a real 8.x response. The legitimate routes the CTO listed
are right; the cheapest is one month of the smallest WHMCS licence on a throwaway VPS (about $20) to produce an
anonymised export and recorded API fixtures.

Capacity: 80 open issues, 16 open PRs, 4 vCPUs, 3,000 Actions minutes with no overage. Review follow-ups are being
filed faster than they are closed (29 today). The three agents in flight on registrars, importer and perf will each
produce a fix round; the budget of 4 agents is already full.

## 5. A Nepali host's first week

Host: 400 clients on WHMCS, cPanel reseller, .com.np and .com, NPR, VAT registered, eSewa and Khalti.

- **Day 1, install.** The docs ask for a Linux host with Docker and 6 GB; the host has a 4 GB VPS at a Nepali
  provider. First leave trigger is cost: an 8 GB VPS is about NPR 5,000–6,500 a month. On a big enough host the
  install is 20 s with HTTPS, a proven smoke path, and readable docs. Mail is `.env` only with no test send.
- **Day 2, company and money.** Company profile, NPR default, VAT 13%, seller PAN on the invoice: all there with docs
  pages, better than WHMCS's tax setup. Then the accountant asks about CBMS and fiscal-year numbering; no answer.
- **Day 2, payments.** eSewa and Khalti settings with docs and official logos. The first real customer whose eSewa
  session expires sees "Pay a different way" (good); the first whose card is declined is stuck (#123).
- **Day 3, clients.** 400 clients cannot be imported. Same leave trigger as rounds 1 and 2. Creating them by hand is
  a day of typing and the cPanel import then links accounts to them well (per-account choices, flags, continue).
- **Day 3, server.** cPanel connect with certificate checklist and trust: better than WHMCS. Package names typed by hand.
- **Day 4, domains.** .com.np by email and portal as before (driver unmerged); .com has no registrar. Every order manual.
- **Day 5, storefront and reports.** No public site (draft). No reports, no credit notes, English only.

What keeps them: ledger and invoice correctness with VAT, the cPanel connection, branded email, the security tier,
readable source and docs. What makes them leave, in order: server cost on day 1, no client import on day 3, CBMS if
it applies, no .np and .com registrar on day 4.
