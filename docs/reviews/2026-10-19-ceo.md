## 2. Are we building the right things in the right order?

Mostly yes: the parity roadmap's wave 3 (panels, registrars, gateways, importer) is the right list, and the
order-to-account chain is the right milestone. Four corrections:

- **The simulator programme is a work-around for a decision that costs under $10.** A Stripe test-mode account is
  free and needs no business. A small VPS with cPanel's trial licence costs a few dollars. Both would retire the
  `stripe` and `whm` simulators' fidelity work (ADR 0016 §2) and give real evidence. Re-ask the owner before S1 and
  S3 are built. If the answer stays no, build `whm` and `stripe` only; defer `khalti` and `esewa` (S5).
- **Stripe (#58) is held to a higher bar than cPanel.** cPanel merged on recorded fixtures and reviews; Stripe
  (2,864 lines, CI green, round-1 fixes in) waits for a simulator that does not exist. Merge it on the same bar,
  labelled "not live-verified", before it drifts further from `develop`.
- **Deployment is over-scoped for a product with zero installs.** Blue-green on one host, expand-then-contract
  checks and a cPanel cron mode (D2, D3) are three PRs of work before the first install. A first host needs D1 plus
  compose, `production.md`, `.env.production.example` and a CI smoke install. Blue-green and cron mode are owner
  decisions from 10-19; recommend revisiting them after the first real deploy, not before.
- **The switcher path has no start date.** No registrar, no WHMCS importer, no cPanel account import. A host with
  existing customers cannot move. Pull cPanel PR 3 (import) ahead of PR 2 (SSO, package select), then the
  registrar prerequisites (#12) and ResellerClub, then the importer.

Cut or keep deferred: Affiliates, social sign-in, AI module, WhatsApp/SMS, e-invoicing, blue-green, cPanel cron
mode, S5 simulators. Keep the home page after the chain above; it is needed for a first host but blocks nobody now.

## 3. Risks to the business

Go-live blockers:

- Nothing has run outside the codespace; `production.md` and `.env.production.example` do not exist, though
  `state.md` item 3 has listed them since 10-18.
- #64: provisioning needs a shared lock store in production; without it, two workers can create one account twice.
- #12: registrations stuck in pending after a killed worker. #25: no key-rotation path.
- #11: the framework security bump is held by the 7-day cooldown until 10-22 (acceptable, scheduled).
- Branch protection on `develop` and `main` is unverified (owner action; the token gets 403).

Compliance:

- Payments: hosted checkout everywhere (Stripe Checkout, Khalti, eSewa), so card data never touches us. Good.
- Tax: GST and VAT invoices exist. India e-invoicing (IRN) and Nepal CBMS are P1 with no date; a host above the
  Indian turnover threshold cannot issue compliant invoices from Mohar. Say so in the pitch until built.
- Data protection: `platform-admin.md` lists GDPR export and deletion as out of scope beyond the client export
  report. A European host will ask in week one.
- No published security policy and no trademark search on "Mohar" (both open since 10-18).

Owner decisions that block the pitch: licence and price model, support model. The two reasons hosts leave WHMCS
(pain-points study §3) are price rises and closed source. Mohar has no answer to either until the owner decides.

Structural: one owner, no named first customer, 108+ agents. Everything is being built for a host nobody has
talked to. That is the largest risk in this review.

## 4. A first paying host's first week

Assume a small Nepal or India host with 300 clients on WHMCS, cPanel servers, selling .com and .np domains.

- **Day 1, install.** `install.md` is sound but asks for PHP 8.4, PostgreSQL 18, an S3-compatible bucket, SMTP,
  cron and Supervisor. The bucket is a surprise (WHMCS needs PHP and MySQL). Behind a proxy, HSTS and the login
  lockout are wrong until D1 merges (`TRUSTED_PROXIES`). Half a day for a sysadmin; a day for a reseller.
- **Day 1, setup.** Company profile, currencies, taxes, brand and email templates are good. Mail transport is
  `.env` only, with no test send (roadmap 3.6).
- **Day 2, servers and products.** The test-connection checklist and certificate trust are better than WHMCS.
  But package names are typed by hand (package select is PR 2), and there is no "Log in to cPanel" for customers.
- **Day 2, the leave trigger.** Their 300 existing accounts cannot be imported from WHM (PR 3) or from WHMCS
  (importer unbuilt). They stop here. Everything below applies only to a host starting from zero.
- **Day 3, domains.** TLD pricing works; every registration is manual (log driver). A host selling domains cannot
  use it.
- **Day 3, payments.** Nepal: Khalti and eSewa, unverified live. Elsewhere: bank transfer only until #58 merges.
- **Day 4, storefront.** No home page; client dashboard and invoice page below the chosen direction; database ids
  visible in places. The host's customers see an unfinished product.
- **Day 5, month-end.** No reports (income, tax liability, aging) and no credit notes. The accountant asks for them.

What keeps them: invoice and ledger correctness, the branded mail, the security tier, admin UI quality, readable
source. What makes them leave: no import, no registrar, no reports.
