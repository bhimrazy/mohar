# Pro-rata and first-period calculations (issue #598, audit L1)

Question from the 10-31 best-practices audit (L1): is `billing/app/Support/Billing/Prorata.php` the single home of the
first-period (pro-rata) calculation, or a leftover to remove? This note records every first-period, pro-rata and
change-plan calculation in `billing/app` on `develop` at `b15e0819`, whether they agree, and what the CTO has to decide.
Nothing in the application changes with it; the PR that adds it only adds tests that pin today's results.

The half-up integer division is written three times: `Prorata::divideRounded`, `Proration::divideHalfUp` and
`Catalog/Support/PriceCalculator::divideRounded`. For positive denominators they return the same value; a probe over
numerators −500..500 and denominators 1..40 found no difference, and `ProrationTest` now pins the first two against each
other.

### Do they agree?

- **Rows 1 and 3 agree exactly** where they overlap. Prorata's partial equals Proration's charge over the cycle that
  ends on the anchor (`Proration::calculate(0, recurring, advance(anchor, −1, day), anchor, orderDate)->charge`). The
  new test checks this for every order date from 2027-12-01 to 2028-12-31, prorata days 1, 15, 28, 29, 30 and 31, and
  monthly, quarterly and annual cycles: no mismatch. They answer different questions (first period vs change of plan)
  and are not copies of each other.
- **Row 2 differs from row 1.** With `prorata_enabled` on, checkout charges what it charges for any product. Example:
  NPR 3,100.00 a month (310000 paisa), prorata day 1, charge-next-month day 20.

  | Order date (Nepal) | Orders today (row 2)                       | `Prorata` / catalog.md §3.4 (row 1)          |
  | ------------------ | ------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
  | 2026-01-10         | 310000, period 01-10 → 02-10               | 220000 (22/31), next due 02-01               |
  | 2026-01-25         | 310000, period 01-25 → 02-25               | 380000 (70000 + 310000), next due 03-01      |
  | 2026-01-31         | 310000, period 01-31 → 02-28               | 320000 (10000 + 310000), next due 03-01      |

- **Row 1 differs from the spec text for longer cycles.** catalog.md §3.4 divides by `days(anchor − 1 month → anchor)`;
  `Prorata` divides by the cycle that ends on the anchor. Annual NPR 36,500.00 (3650000) ordered 2026-01-10, day 1: the
  spec text gives 3650000 × 22/31 = 2590323 for the partial, `Prorata` gives 3650000 × 22/365 = 220000. The spec's
  worked examples (AC 13, 14) are monthly only, where the two are the same.

So the staff setting "Prorata" on a product (Admin → Catalog → Products) is saved and shown but does nothing, and
`Prorata` is the only implementation of the first-period rule. It has no copies to move onto it.

### Edge cases pinned

`ProrataTest` and `ProrationTest` now pin, in minor units: the last day of January; leap and common February (2000 of
2900 over 29 days, 1968 over 28); prorata day 31 clamped to April 30 and the day-30 period that starts on a clamped
February 28; orders on the anchor day, leap day 29 and the charge-next-month day itself; half-up at exactly .5 (101 ×
14/28 → 51); a zero-decimal currency (JPY 1000 × 22/31 → 710); quarterly and annual denominators including a leap
year; and the order date read in Asia/Kathmandu.

Time zone: both calculators count calendar days of the dates they are given, in those dates' own zone. The company
clock's `today()` (00:00 Nepal time) and `todayDate()` (00:00 UTC on the company date) give the same result. The same
Nepal-midnight instant converted to UTC is still the previous day and charges one day more (2300 instead of 2200 on
3100). A daylight-saving change does not lose a day (New York, March 2026: 27 of 31 either way). Checkout already uses
`$clock->today()` and the upgrade path `todayDate()`, so neither is affected today; a caller that wires `Prorata` must
pass one of those, not a UTC instant.

## Recommendation

Neither (a) nor (b) as worded: there is one first-period implementation and nothing to consolidate onto it.

- **The home is Orders, not the kernel.** catalog.md §3.4 says the calculation is "performed by Orders using Catalog
  settings", Orders would be its only caller, and the inputs already reach Orders through `ProductSummary`. Change-plan
  proration stays in `Services/Support/Proration` (services.md §5), where it is used and correct.
- **Wiring it changes behaviour** (row 2 → row 1), so this PR does not. When the CTO chooses D1 below, one PR moves
  `Prorata` to `app/Modules/Orders/Pricing/`, calls it from checkout (first amount, invoice period, the service's
  anchor day and next invoice date), and deletes `App\Support\Billing\Prorata`. That deletion is a kernel API change:
  it needs `php artisan kernel:api-snapshot`, a `kernel-changelog.d/` fragment, a `changelog.d/` fragment (first
  invoices of prorata products change) and an ADR 0008 amendment, which names the kernel as its home today.

## Decisions for the CTO

1. **Wire pro-rata at checkout or drop it for v1.** D1: implement catalog.md §3.4 in Orders as above (catalog spec
   SHOULD; staff can already turn it on). D2: drop it for v1, hide the product's prorata fields in the admin so the
   setting no longer promises something, and delete `Prorata` and its test.
2. **Denominator for longer cycles.** Keep `Prorata`'s cycle length (recommended: a quarter, half-year or year price is
   divided by that period's days) and correct the spec text, or follow the spec text as written.
3. **Addons `prorata_with_parent`** (row 4). Implement with D1, or hide it with D2.
4. **Optional:** one kernel half-up division (for example on `App\Support\Money`) to replace the three helpers. They
   agree today, so this is tidiness, not a fix; it is a kernel change of its own.

Related detail for whoever wires D1: `EloquentServiceRegistry`'s docblock reads `ServiceDraft::$nextDueDate` as the end
of the first period, but `PlaceOrder` passes the order date, so a new service gets `next_invoice_date` = the order date
until its first invoice is paid. With pro-rata the first period ends on the anchor, so that value has to be set right.
