## High

### H1. A chargeback of a payment that went to account credit leaves the credit in place

- **Where:**
  - `billing/app/Modules/Billing/Actions/RecordChargeback.php:47`: `$amount = min($amount, $payment->refundable_remaining)`.
  - `billing/app/Modules/Billing/Actions/RecordChargeback.php:83`: credit is reversed only for `InvoiceKind::AddFunds`.
  - `billing/app/Modules/Billing/Services/GatewayCallbackHandler.php:365-388` (`reversed()`).
- **Impact:** When a payment goes to account credit, its `refundable_remaining` shrinks by the credited amount. This
  happens for an overpayment, a second payment of an invoice that is already paid, or a late payment
  (`ApplyPayment.php:106-107`, `:126-129`). A dispute is then booked only up to the invoice-applied part, so a dispute
  of a payment that went wholly to credit books a chargeback of 0. The credit stays spendable: the client can apply it
  to invoices, and invoice generation can apply it automatically.
  - The attack is friendly fraud: pay an invoice twice (two tabs, or a session completed late) or overpay, then dispute
    the charge with the bank. The client gets the money back and keeps the credit.
  - Nothing in the app asks staff to act. The attempt's late or credit to-do may already be marked checked, and the
    dispute raises no new one.
  - #152 added exactly this take-back for provider *refunds* of flagged hosted attempts (`takeBackCredit()`), so the
    rule is already accepted. Disputes are the missing twin.
- **Reproduction (probes H1a and H1b, both fail on SQLite and PostgreSQL):**
  - **H1a:** pay invoice 5,000 by webhook (`h1_ch_1`), then pay it again (`h1_ch_2`; the result is "payment recorded on
    closed invoice", credit 5,000). A `charge.dispute` for `h1_ch_2` returns 200 "chargeback recorded". The credit
    balance is still 5,000.
  - **H1b:** overpay 8,000 on a 5,000 invoice (credit 3,000), then dispute 8,000. The credit is still 3,000.
- **Fix:**
  1. In `reversed()`, compute what the payments could not absorb (`$remaining` after the loop). Debit that from the
     client's credit through the same code as `takeBackCredit()`: under the client lock, keyed by
     `(gateway, dispute id)` on the `credit_ledger` unique index, and allowing a negative balance as add-funds
     reversals already do (`allowNegative: true`), because the money has already left.
  2. Whatever cannot be taken back (credit already spent, if the balance must not go negative) gets a staff to-do:
     set `check_reason` (a new `CHECK_DISPUTE`) on the attempt when there is one, and log `billing.payment.disputed`
     otherwise.
  3. Drop the `flaggedAttempt()` precondition for this path. Every credited portion of a disputed charge came from
     that charge, whether or not a hosted attempt was flagged.
  4. Adopt H1a and H1b. Add a replay test (the same dispute twice takes credit back once) and a race test against the
     unique index, like `LatePaymentSecurityProbesTest`.

## Medium

### M1. A provider refund of an unflagged overpayment ends as a log line, and the credit stays

- **Where:** `billing/app/Modules/Billing/Services/GatewayCallbackHandler.php:278-285`. When `flaggedAttempt()` finds
  nothing, `refunded()` answers "refund needs staff review" and writes only a `gateway_log` row.
- **Impact:** Refunds are started by staff (for example from the Stripe Dashboard), so this is not client-driven. But
  someone who refunds the whole charge at the provider usually does not know that part of it became account credit
  here. The client then gets the refund and keeps the credit, and no to-do, flag or dashboard count points at it.
  Webhook overpayments and direct (non-hosted) payments on closed invoices never get a `check_reason`, so they always
  take this path.
- **Reproduction (probe H1c, fails):** overpay 8,000 on a 5,000 invoice, then send a `charge.refunded` of 8,000. The
  result is "refund needs staff review", and the credit is still 3,000.
- **Fix:** Use the H1 take-back for every refund the payments cannot absorb, not only for flagged attempts. At the
  least, raise a staff to-do (an activity entry with an event the dashboard counts) instead of a log-only outcome.

## Low

### L1. Audit descriptions still interpolate staff-entered names

`billing/app/Modules/Services/Actions/ImportServerAccounts.php:134,197,278` and
`billing/app/Modules/Services/Jobs/FetchServerAccounts.php:85,96` put `{$server->name}` and `{$service->label()}` into
the description, against standards §4 (fixed sentences, values in properties). This continues the baseline's L5 into
the new import code. #89 fixed `SaveServer`, but these are new. The values are sanitised and truncated, so the effect is
cosmetic and on search. **Fix:** use fixed sentences and move `server` and `service` into properties.

### L2. The docs site is outside every dependency gate

`docs-site/` has its own `package-lock.json`, but `.github/dependabot.yml` lists npm for `/billing` only. CI's
`npm audit` and `check-dependency-age.mjs` run in `billing/` only. `npm audit` there reports 11 moderate advisories, all
from `postcss-selector-parser` <7.1.6 (GHSA-rj75-hqrm-r3gf, quadratic CPU on crafted selectors) through
astro/starlight/expressive-code. They are build-time only and the input is our own content, so there is no runtime
exposure. **Fix:** add an npm entry for `/docs-site` to Dependabot (same cooldown), and run `npm audit` and the age check
on `docs-site/` in CI when it changes. Bump once a patched starlight chain is past the cooldown. The same gap applies to
the images in `deploy/compose.prod.yaml` (caddy, postgres, rustfs): Dependabot's docker-compose entry covers `/billing`
only, so Caddy and PostgreSQL security releases are not proposed. Add `/deploy` to it.

### L3. The import review page writes state on GET

`ServerImportController::review()` records the reviewed rows and their digest (`AccountImportPlan::review()`) on a GET.
The apply POST is CSRF-protected and must send that digest, so a cross-site navigation cannot import anything. At most
it replaces the stored review, and the next apply then fails closed. **Fix (optional):** leave it as is, or make it
a POST. Say so in the docblock either way.

### L4. In the production compose stack, the outbound policy allows the stack's own private network

`OUTBOUND_ALLOW_PRIVATE_ADDRESSES` defaults to true (ADR 0015), and the app container shares the `backend` network with
`postgres:5432` and `rustfs:9000`. Today's outbound callers cannot speak to them: WHM requires TLS, webhooks always
refuse private ranges, and gateways use fixed hosts. A future driver with plain HTTP and a staff-entered host could.
The docs-site checklist already says to set it to false when staff are not fully trusted. **Fix:** consider defaulting
it to false in `.env.production.example`, with a comment that a panel on the LAN needs true.

## Verified secure

- **Baseline findings, all fixed by #89 and pinned by tests:**
  - B1: `support.inbound_secret` is `secret: true` and encrypted by a migration. A registry-wide `SecretSettingsTest`
    requires every credential-like setting to be secret and its section to sit behind a sensitive permission.
  - M1: retargeting a server clears stored credentials and pins, and the move is audited (`ServerRetargetTest`).
  - M2: `register`, `password-reset-link` and `password-reset` limiters (`AuthThrottleTest`).
  - L1: the behavioural step-up scan over every admin non-GET route, with a two-item exemption list that has a
    vacuity guard.
  - L2: webhooks pin through `OutboundAddressGuard::pin()` with proxy bypass, and private ranges stay refused.
  - L3: the client password route has `throttle:6,1`.
  - L4: registrar requests keep secrets out of old input, and update and test require step-up.
- **Late payments (#152):**
  - A closed attempt settles only through `lookup()` under the attempt row lock, with the same txn-id, currency and
    amount guards.
  - The anonymous return route re-looks-up a closed attempt only inside the late window and at most once a minute. The
    hourly sweep has a cap, a deadline and a per-gateway breaker.
  - The refund take-back is exactly once per `(gateway, refund id)`: a check under the client lock plus a partial
    unique index (built `CONCURRENTLY` on PostgreSQL). A race surfaces as `UniqueConstraintViolationException` after
    the whole transaction rolls back, and is answered "duplicate".
  - Refund rows keep `unique(gateway, gateway_txn_id, type)`, and attempts keep `unique(gateway, provider_txn_id)`.
  - `late-checked` needs `billing.payments.manage` plus step-up, and is a conditional update (one audit entry).
  - `LatePaymentSecurityProbesTest` passes on PostgreSQL.
- **Stripe:**
  - Every `v1` signature is compared with `hash_equals`; `v0` is ignored, the header length is capped and the timestamp
    tolerance enforced.
  - The handler also rejects sends older than 5 minutes and caps the body at 256 KB before any signature work.
  - Only the event type and object id are read from the body; the object is then fetched from Stripe, and `livemode`
    is checked against the gateway mode.
  - Admin refunds carry an idempotency key and are tagged, so their webhooks do not double-book.
  - `StripeAmount` converts with integer arithmetic only and refuses amounts that do not convert exactly.
- **Account import (#95, #101, #119, #133, #153):**
  - `services.servers.import` is sensitive, so wildcards never grant it, and every mutating import route needs step-up
    and has a throttle.
  - Choices accept only usernames from the fetched list, active clients (and only for staff with `core.clients.view`)
    and products that provision on this server. Apply imports exactly the reviewed rows, bound by a digest.
  - The `(server_id, username)` live-unique index stops a double import.
  - The fetched list is `encrypted:array` and `#[Hidden]`, and fetch errors are masked with the connection's secrets
    before `report()`.
  - Owner matching fails closed: an unknown owner or connection user never counts as ours.
  - **Email-takeover defence:** a client whose email changed in the last 30 days, or whose self-set address no
    verified login holds, is never proposed or bulk-ticked. `email_source` is set on the model `saving` hook, and a
    change that declares no source counts as the client's own. No query-builder `update()` of `clients.email` exists.
    Profile email changes reset `email_verified_at`. An invitation marks the address verified only when it matches the
    address the link was mailed to.
- **Invitations:**
  - Only a SHA-256 hash of a 48-character token is stored.
  - Single use under a row lock. A link is void after another email, a password, 2FA, a sign-in or removed access.
  - The accept page sends `no-referrer`, and is throttled to 30/min for GET and 10/min for POST. A user with 2FA is
    never logged in past the second factor.
  - The link goes through `SensitiveValue` and the template link policy.
- **Automation (#122):** "Run now" needs `core.automation.run`, takes the unique lock itself and refuses while a run
  holds the shared lock. Lock release is CLI-only, asks for confirmation and is audited. The automation connection's
  `retry_after` (3,900 s) is above the job timeout (3,600 s), so a long run is not handed to a second worker.
- **Deploy (#80):**
  - Only Caddy publishes ports. PostgreSQL and RustFS sit on an `internal` network.
  - The PHP containers run with a read-only root, `cap_drop: ALL` and `no-new-privileges`, as a non-root user.
    `TRUSTED_PROXIES` names only the edge subnet.
  - The Caddy access and error logs replace token path segments (reset, staff and account invitation, email verify)
    and every query string with REDACTED, in the URI and in `Location`, and drop `Referer`. Cookies and Authorization
    are redacted by Caddy itself. This is pinned by `ProductionAccessLogLocationTest`, `ProductionComposeHardeningTest`
    and `ProductionEdgeTrustTest`.
  - The image is built `--no-dev`, and the dockerignore plus a build-time `find` keep any `.env` out of it. The env
    example says `chmod 600` and keeps `APP_KEY` out of backups.
- **Simulators (#100, #113, #115):**
  - Require-dev only, and every `simulate:*` command refuses outside `local` and `testing`.
  - Servers bind 127.0.0.1. Each scenario has its own CA, name-constrained to 127.0.0.1 and its DNS name. The control
    plane is on its own port behind a token.
  - Endpoint overrides are honoured only in local and testing, are https only, carry no credentials, are logged
    critical elsewhere, and are pinned through the guard. Loopback is reachable only in development environments
    (fail closed).
  - The e2e command and its detached guard drop only names matching `^billing_e2e_[0-9a-f]{8}$`, so they can never drop
    a development or preview database.
  - No live keys are committed; only simulator constants are.
- **Docs site (#137):** a static Astro build that is not published. `.npmrc` sets `ignore-scripts` and a 7-day minimum
  release age, and the deploy script only force-pushes `dist/` to a remote the operator names.
- **Outbound calls:** Stripe goes through `Endpoints::for()`, WHM through `OutboundAddressGuard` with pin, and
  webhooks through the guard plus stricter rules. No new outbound call bypasses the guard.
- **Secrets in jobs and logs:** no queued job or notification constructor carries a secret. `SendEmailMessage` and
  `RunModuleCommand` are `ShouldBeEncrypted`. Gateway raw payloads go through `GatewayLogger::redact`.
- **Mail:** templates add no external links. One-time links are placed by the system only, through the
  `SecretTemplatePolicy` fields.
- **Uploads:** Support attachments are unchanged since the baseline, which verified them.
- **CI:** `pull_request` only (no `pull_request_target`), `contents: read`, actions pinned by SHA. The cooldown waiver
  needs a label, which needs write access.
- **Mass assignment and SQL:** new models use `#[Fillable]`. Staff client creation uses `forceCreate` on validated data
  plus server-set `origin` and `email_source`. The two new `whereRaw` calls are parameterised.
- **Dependencies:** `composer audit` found no advisories. `npm audit` in `billing/` found 0 vulnerabilities (with and
  without dev dependencies). For `docs-site/`, see L2.
